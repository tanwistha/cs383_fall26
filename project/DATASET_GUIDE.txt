Campus Delivery Project -- Dataset Guide
==========================================

FILES
-----
  buildings.csv    18 campus buildings: id, name, type, x/y coordinates (meters)
  pathways.csv     32 walkways between buildings: distance (m) and walk time (s)
  deliveries.csv   30 delivery requests (see below)

Every team uses the SAME three files. That's on purpose: since
everyone starts from identical data, differences in your results come
from your design choices, not from luck of the draw. That's also what
makes it fair to compare results across teams at the end of the
semester.


FIXED CONSTANTS (not in the CSVs -- just use these numbers)
-------------------------------------------------------------
  Delivery agent starts at:  B16  (Poplar Mail Center)
  Carrying capacity:         15 kg at any one time
  Note: capacity limits what you're carrying AT ONCE, not in total.
  Weight leaves your bag when you drop something off, so capacity
  mainly affects how many pickups you can batch before you deliver.


DELIVERIES.CSV COLUMNS
-------------------------
  request_id          unique id, e.g. R01
  pickup, dropoff      building ids
  priority             HIGH / NORMAL / LOW
  size_kg              package size/weight
  window_start_min,
  window_end_min       optional delivery time window, in minutes from
                        the start of the shift. Blank means no window.


THREE SIZES TO TEST ON
-------------------------
Don't ask us for three separate files -- just use the first N rows of
deliveries.csv, in order:

  SMALL   first  8 requests   (rows R01-R08)
  MEDIUM  first 16 requests   (rows R01-R16)
  FULL    all   30 requests   (rows R01-R30)

SMALL is intentionally brute-forceable in its simplest form: if you
handle requests one at a time (finish a pickup and its dropoff before
starting the next), there are only 8! = 40,320 possible orderings --
solvable by exhaustive search in well under a second. Use it to see
how close your solver gets to that baseline.

Careful: that "one at a time" optimum is a useful baseline, not
necessarily the best possible plan. Since capacity allows carrying more
than one item at once, a solver that interleaves pickups and dropoffs
(carry two things, then drop them both off) can sometimes beat it. If
your solver does better than the "one at a time" brute-force number,
that's not a bug -- that's a real finding worth reporting.

You are required to produce a working solution for MEDIUM and FULL.
Running on all three sizes and reporting what changes as N grows is
part of the assignment -- see the handout.
